• Why can high proportional gain cause oscillation or overshoot?
    When the joint reaches the target, the error is zero, so the P-control torque is zero. But the joint is still moving, because the torque has been accelerating it the whole way in. Because of the arm’s moment of inertia, it can’t stop instantly. After it passes the target, the error changes sign and the torque reverses. The torque now slows the joint down, but that takes time, so the joint keeps going past the target. This is the overshoot. A higher Kp gives a larger torque on the way in, so the joint arrives faster and overshoots further. It then swings back and overshoots the other way, which is oscillation. P control only pushes based on position, and this joint has almost no damping or friction, so very little energy is removed on each swing.

 • What does damping add to a joint controller?
    The damping term -Kd * velocity always pushes against the joint’s motion. While the joint moves toward the target, it opposes the P term, so the net torque is smaller and the joint builds up less speed. After the joint passes the target, the damping term and the P term both point back toward the target and work together to stop it. In this way damping removes kinetic energy throughout the motion, so the joint arrives with less speed, which reduces overshoot and stops the oscillation. Damping can be too large: then the joint doesn’t overshoot, but it moves slowly and takes a long time to reach the target (overdamped). A good Kd balances fast motion against little overshoot. Damping is zero when the joint is at rest, so it can’t fix the steady error caused by gravity.

•	How do I distinguish a controller problem from a model/actuator problem?
    your controller  →  actuator  →  physics (gravity, friction, limits)
     computes ctrl      turns ctrl     decides how the joint
                         into torque    actually moves
    I check each layer in turn and change one thing at a time. First, I confirm the actuator type, so I know ctrl really means torque; otherwise no gain will fix the behaviour. Second, if the joint moves the wrong way, I compare the sign of the control with the sign of the error. If they disagree, the controller is wrong; if they agree, the problem is in the model (gear, axis, or a too-small torque limit). Third, for a steady error, I test a prediction: with P control at rest, error = gravity torque / Kp. On the shoulder, the error matched this prediction at three Kp values and almost vanished with gravity turned off, so it’s caused by gravity, not a bug. Fourth, I compare data.ctrl with data.actuator_force to confirm the actuator applied what I commanded.
